Measured spectra · independent test campaign

JAXA NAL-735 · Noshiro static firing

A 260 kN solid rocket tested in December 2007. Fukuda et al. (2011), Figures 17–18, publish measured spectra at M1 (80° arc) and M8 (near field), together with two model-based ground corrections. These are experimental observations, separate from the Smith prediction replay.

Why this case is useful—and what remains uncertain
  1. Published motor inputs: thrust 260 kN, exit diameter 0.614 m, exit Mach 2.92. Tsutsumi et al. (2009), Table 1, also report overexpansion Pe/Pa = 0.418 and fully expanded diameter/Mach of 0.446 m / 2.47. The baseline consistently uses exit diameter/Mach with the Eldred core.
  2. Derived and assumed inputs: exit velocity ≈2,456 m/s follows from the published frequency/Strouhal mapping (4,000 Hz at Sr = 1). Ambient sound speed 343 m/s is assumed. Acoustic efficiency 0.84% is the companion paper’s Sutherland-based estimate, not a fit here. We use the calculator’s ½FU power convention; the paper uses fully expanded kinetic power, so this is an assessment of our documented model, not exact replication of their prediction.
  3. Geometry: M1 lies on a 39 m radius at 80° from the downstream axis. M8 is 10 m downstream and 13 m laterally offset. Nozzle/microphone heights are 1.8 m and 1.45 / 0.12 m. Height differences are folded into the radial distance for this straight axisymmetric plume. The 2011 prose calls 39 m a diameter; the 2009 paper and diagrams identify it as the radius.
  4. Fixed assessment choices: DSM-II, Eldred core, normalized historical source curves, legacy directivity, 240 slices, zero reflection, no extra propagation loss and no design margin. The published ground corrections are retained as data; no second reflection adjustment is applied.
  5. Scope: two receivers and 13 assessed bands each provide a real test of spectral shape and spatial prediction. Ground correction, overexpanded flow, acoustic-efficiency uncertainty and empirical near-field limitations prevent a clean universal accuracy claim. Above 2 kHz, retained readings are diagnostic only.
