W = (η/100) N F U / 2   [W]
Lw = 10 log₁₀(W / 10⁻¹²)   [dB re 1 pW]
Lp,b = 10 log₁₀ Σₛ { (Ws,b / 10⁻¹²) / (4πrₛ²) × 10^[(DIₛ,b + Aₛ,b + C)/10] }
Lp,overall = 10 log₁₀ Σᵦ 10^(Lp,b / 10)

The standard SP-8072 pressure/intensity reference convention is retained (ρc ≈ 400 Pa·s/m); 10 log₁₀(4π) = 10.992 dB replaces the report's rounded 11 dB. No atmospheric-impedance correction is applied.

Heritage models

  • DSM-I: Sr = f D/U. Integrate Figure 5's density across each band; place the band at the Figure 14 solid-line apparent source position. That historical preferred curve includes open-scoop data and is not a pure universal free-jet fit.
  • DSM-II: Lc = 3.45 D (1 + 0.38 M)². Use Figure 12 over 0.1–5 Lc, with midpoint slices, and Figure 13 using modified Sr = f x / (M c₀).
  • Normalization: independently normalize the full spectral distributions and the truncated axial distribution when selected. The displayed band range is never normalized to contain all power. Turn normalization off for a closer raw-curve comparison.
  • Digitization: Figures 5, 10, 12, 13 and 14 are approximate readings of scanned curves, not numerical measurements. Allow roughly 1 dB of curve-reading precision and about 10% in location, in addition to model uncertainty.

Research options

  • Varnier / Haynes–Kenny: alternative core coefficient 1.75. For DSM-II only, an idealized bend and optional core termination are available. Engine-specific RSRM directivity is not included.
  • SATURN stationary adaptation: uses Mathews' piecewise spectral density, s/D = 9.85 Sr⁻⁰·²¹, and the released Atlas V directivity table. It does not include launch trajectories, retarded time or plume-emergence masking.
  • SATURN propagation: uses the pinned code's 16 Hz and 8000 Hz loss parameters. The dissertation's printed equations differ; the audit records those discrepancies.
  • Modern directivity domain: the released table covers only 0–90° from downstream. Out-of-angle predictions are rejected; the map leaves those cells blank. Sr outside the table is held at its boundary and flagged.

What the literature changes—and what it does not establish

  1. 2001 / 2009: shorter cores and deflector effects. Varnier and Haynes–Kenny motivate core shortening and core termination for specific configurations. These are exposed as separate sensitivity controls.
  2. 2016: full-scale evidence challenges a universal shorter core. James and colleagues found overall-level support for the original core relationship across four full-scale solid motors, while spectra remained imperfect. A shorter core is therefore not the default “accuracy upgrade.”
  3. 2021: an inherited source-distribution error. Gee traced SP-8072 Figure 12 to a plotting error in earlier jet experiments. The corrected laboratory-jet distribution cannot by itself establish a universal hot-rocket distribution. DSM-II is retained and clearly labeled as historical; no invented correction curve is substituted.
  4. 2022–2024: source physics and installation matter. Reviews and measurements emphasize distributed/coherent sources, nonlinear propagation, impingement, ground effects, and water suppression. These cannot reliably be represented by a universal dB correction.
  5. 2025: modern measured spectrum and directivity. Mathews' SATURN framework provides published formulas and a reusable dataset. Its reported launch-validation errors depend on the complete model and datasets; they do not transfer to this stationary implementation.

Limits that affect interpretation

  • Stationary chemical-rocket broadband mixing-noise estimate. No ignition overpressure, discrete tones, combustion instability, shock-associated-noise separation, structural response or transient peak pressure.
  • No nonlinear propagation, refraction, terrain, shielding, water-injection physics, weather profile, ground impedance or coherent interference model.
  • Merged clusters use D√N. Independent clusters sum uncorrelated, co-located engine powers using D. Neither resolves actual nozzle spacing or azimuthal cluster asymmetry.
  • A-weighted levels are band-center estimates. “Overall” always means the selected band edges, not all frequencies. The map uses the same band scope.
  • Reflection choices are idealized +0, +3.01 or +6.02 dB. They are mutually exclusive; applying both ground doubling and pressure doubling would double-count effects.
  • Core-length controls affect DSM-II only. Bent centerlines do not constitute validated deflector or launch-pad simulation.
