  1. Different directivity: Smith uses Wilby III; the calculator baseline uses Eldred’s Figure 10. These are different empirical models. Smith extends Wilby below its stated 90°–180° domain.
  2. Angle error in the appended code: Appendix D, printed p. 262, uses a one-argument arctangent that loses the quadrant. Its straight-jet calculation gives angles above 180° for this downstream receiver and incorrectly activates Wilby’s low-frequency correction intended for angles ≥170°. Our calculator uses the plume tangent and receiver vector, giving angles in 0°–180°.
  3. Smaller conventions: unnormalized analytical fits and center-density × base-2 bandwidth account for the remaining difference. They are reproduced only in this audit.

The close match strongly implicates the published angle calculation, but the original saved run is unavailable. This is a reconstruction of its behavior, not an exact execution of the dissertation software. Correcting that angle alone would move the low-frequency result away from the printed curve.

Inputs, assumptions and comparison limits
  1. Published inputs: thrust 794.3 N; velocity 406.6 m/s; diameter 0.073 m; Mach 1.15; one nozzle; acoustic power 14 W (Table 9). The equivalent efficiency is 0.008670%; this encodes the paper’s power assumption.
  2. Geometry: free jet; receiver (+3.5, 3.5) m in our coordinates, corresponding to the paper’s Z = −3.5 m. Its nozzle distance is 4.950 m; 3.5 m is the transverse offset. Sound speed 353.50 m/s is derived from 311 K using ideal air.
  3. Workbench choices: Eldred core, normalized historical curves, 240 slices over 0.1–5 core lengths, no reflection or extra propagation loss. The audit retains that source grid. Smith’s exact saved small-jet discretization is unavailable. Appendix D’s external Proudman data branch is consistent with the later CFD study (Section 5.3, printed p. 123); it is not needed to reconstruct the Chapter 3 curve with the empirical fits.
  4. What this checks: published-curve reconstruction and discrepancy attribution. The baseline’s 13.11 dB value is an RMS difference across bands, not a uniform offset or an OASPL error. No error was found in the calculator’s angle or power summation. The low-speed jet is outside standard chemical-rocket conditions, and Table 10’s broader-band OASPL is not an acceptance target.
