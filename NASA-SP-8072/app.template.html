Technical assurance

Verification is layered

Equation checks, numerical cross-checks and measured validation answer different questions. The distinction is preserved here.

Published numerical benchmark

NASA Stennis' 2011 report, Table 3, gives acoustic power 10.58 MW and sound power level 190.25 dB re 1 pW. This is a directly reproducible check of the power-to-level conversion.

Independent numerical verification

  • A separate Python/SciPy reference integrates spectra by adaptive quadrature and interpolates the original released SATURN directivity data.
  • Fixed frequency/location cases check spectrum normalization, source positions, geometric spreading, angle conventions, directivity, empirical propagation and pressure summation.
  • Automated invariants check thrust/efficiency scaling, inverse-square spreading, reflection corrections, cluster conventions, frequency coverage, DSM-II slice convergence and input rejection.
  • Browser checks exercise offline startup, each model, all charts, case round trips, stale results, exports and narrow-screen layout.

Secondary technical audit

  1. Power and reference units: η is a percentage in the interface and a fraction in the equation. W uses total engine power; dB references are 1 pW and 20 µPa.
  2. Published equation discrepancies: Mathews Eq. 6.5 prints a negative de-normalization expression and Eq. 6.10 adds a positive spreading term. This implementation follows dimensional analysis and the released code: add source level, subtract spreading loss.
  3. Propagation version: the released code uses f₁ = 16 Hz, n₁ = √(r/12000), f₂ = 8000 Hz, n₂ = (r/3000)¹·⁵. The dissertation prints different parameters. No silent mixture of those formulations is used.
  4. Band energy: spectral densities are integrated over exact base-10 third-octave edges; they are not mistaken for band powers. All sources are summed in linear energy.
  5. Domain controls: unsupported modern directivity angles produce errors or masked map cells, rather than uncontrolled extrapolation. Source coincidences are rejected.
  6. Known heritage uncertainty: the Figure 12 error and manual digitization remain explicit limitations. Normalization fixes energy balance, not the underlying physical source distribution.

Experimental cases found

  • NASA Stennis 2011: a 400 klbf LOX/RP engine with published input ratios and measured/predicted spectra. The paper reports DSM-I near-field levels around 143.1 dB and DSM-II around 149.5 dB. Exact flow dimensions, deflector geometry, ad-hoc source placement and custom curves are incomplete, so those receiver levels are not presented as a passed replication.
  • Haynes–Kenny 2009: Saturn V measured spectra at vehicle stations 85 m and 45 m from the nozzle compare full and terminated cores. Useful physical evidence, but insufficient complete inputs for an exact independently reproducible case here.
  • Mathews 2025: Firefly Alpha and Starship launch comparisons provide modern experimental evidence. Raw flight trajectories and all measurement histories are not in the pinned repository. This stationary application does not claim that validation.

Release status: numerical/software verification supports correct execution of the documented model. End-to-end experimental validation for an actual engine and facility remains open. See the delivered technical audit for test results and reproducibility instructions.
