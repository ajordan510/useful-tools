Vibration Damage Comparison

Reconstruct the heritage sine exposure. Compare it with a random requirement at each candidate resonance.

Methodology overview

Primary equation reference: Zorman, Slavič & Boltežar (2023), Vibration fatigue by spectral methods—A review with open-source support, Section 3.2, equations 29–32 (Dirlik method).

Both environments are passed through the same bank of linear single-degree-of-freedom oscillators to compare relative-displacement fatigue severity. Heritage exposure accumulates cycles from the specified sine dwells (or one independent assumed resonant dwell per oscillator) and optional quasi-steady sweeps. Random exposure uses expected damage from a stationary Gaussian response: Dirlik is the default, with narrowband/Rayleigh available as a cross-check or governing method. The heritage/random damage ratio supports an exposure-coverage assessment when the dynamics and displacement-to-stress relationship are unchanged.

The cited paper supplies the random-fatigue formulation; the sine-test reconstruction and SDOF comparison workflow are this tool's implementation. FLife provides numerical verification. Irvine's example is a separate comparison, not the governing equation source. Review the separate peak screen and the assumptions under Method & interpretation before making a qualification determination.

03 / NEW RANDOM REQUIREMENT

PSD and exposure

frequency_Hz, PSD_g²_per_Hz
How to choose k · VRS and extreme response

Start with the analysis or qualification requirement. If it prescribes k, use that value and record its source in Evidence and assumptions. The default k = 4 is an editable screening assumption, not a universal recommendation.

VRS: the random RMS response of each oscillator versus natural frequency. Here it is relative displacement in mm, using the same Q and response coordinate as the sine response. Comparing RMS directly with a sine peak mixes different statistics.

Peak screen: k × RMS. For zero-mean Gaussian response, RMS = σ. Although ±3σ contains about 99.73% of instantaneous samples, that is not the probability that a complete exposure stays below 3σ. Longer exposure and more response cycles increase the opportunity for larger peaks.

Extreme response spectrum (ERS): use a duration-dependent peak model when assessing the maximum over an event. State whether it estimates a typical maximum or a chosen non-exceedance probability. These are different results. A typical-maximum estimate is not a high-confidence limit.

For a stationary, nearly narrowband Gaussian response, a common leading estimate is k ≈ √[2 ln(ν₀T)], with response zero-upcrossing rate ν₀ in Hz and duration T in seconds. It requires many cycles; ν₀ is approximately fₙ near resonance. At 200 Hz over 60 seconds it gives about 4.33. This is only an indicative extreme level, not a prescribed k or probability bound.

Use the applicable exposure duration, a justified response bandwidth model, and the required non-exceedance probability to select a design peak. Consider measured response crest factors or time-history simulation for non-Gaussian excitation, transients or correlated peaks. Probability at one oscillator does not establish simultaneous coverage of every possible mode.

Sources: VRS definition; Irvine, Peak Response to Random Vibration, Rev H. External references open only when selected.

PSD interpolates log-log and is zero outside the supplied band. +3 dB nearly doubles PSD. Use the specified test duration or justified equivalent service duration. Do not add a qualification margin twice.

ENGINEERING BASIS

What this tool answers

For the specified Q, fatigue exponent, axis, exposure and modal scope: is the modeled heritage fatigue severity at least as large as the modeled new random severity? A separate comparison asks whether the selected random peak-response screen exceeds the heritage response.

A ratio above the requested target is evidence of modeled exposure coverage. A ratio below it identifies a coverage gap to investigate. Neither result alone establishes hardware qualification.

Available comparison methods

Method Appropriate use In this release
Fatigue-damage spectrum (FDS) Compare unlike base excitations with limited component response data. Implemented with relative-displacement SDOF oscillators.
Measured / FEM stress response Known critical locations, correlated transfer functions and fatigue data. Recommended follow-on; stress FRF import is not implemented.
Time histories + rainflow + Miner Measured stress, transient sweeps or cases needing direct cycle counting. Follow-on method; simulation and rainflow are not implemented.
gRMS / equal energy / peak matching Input and peak sanity checks. Not accepted as fatigue equivalence.

1. Normalize the heritage profile

xpeak = DA / 2
apeak = (2πf)² xpeak = 2πf vpeak
apeak = √2 aRMS   (sine only);   g0 = 9.80665 m/s²

All calculations use sine peak amplitude. A PSD ordinate is density, not a sinusoidal G amplitude. Velocity peak/RMS and acceleration peak/RMS/peak-to-peak must be identified explicitly.

2. Filter through the same oscillator

|Hz/a| = 1 / √[(ωn² − ω²)² + (2ζωnω)²]
Sz(f) = g0² |Hz/a|² Sa(f);   ζ = 1/(2Q)

Relative displacement z is a fatigue stress proxy. The unknown stress-per-displacement coefficient cancels only when the same mode, stress relationship and fatigue law apply to both cases. Bank points are independent hypothetical oscillators; their damage must not be summed across frequency.

3. Accumulate cycles consistently

I = Σ ni(zpeak,i / zref)b,   zref = 1 mm
tdwell = min(tcap, Ncap/fdwell)
Isweep = ∫ f [zpeak(f)/zref]b (dt/df) df

I is a normalized damage index. It is not an absolute Miner fraction. The 1 mm reference is numerical normalization, not an allowable. Heritage index is the sum of separate sequential sweeps and dwells. A stationary random response uses spectral moments mj = ∫ fjSz(f) df with frequency in Hz.

In assumed-resonance mode, each SDOF receives one dwell at its own natural frequency with the local sine-profile amplitude. Its duration is the earlier positive time/cycle cap, with repeats applied independently. No neighboring hypothetical dwell contributes damage or peak response. Outside the sine profile there is no dwell credit. Profile jumps require an explicit boundary rule. Enabled sweeps and the full random PSD still act on each oscillator.

4. Estimate random fatigue

ν0 = √(m2/m0),   νp = √(m4/m2)
INB = Tν0(√(2m0)/zref)b Γ(1+b/2)

Dirlik estimates rainflow-amplitude statistics from m₀, m₁, m₂ and m₄ and is the default. The narrowband formula provides a transparent cross-check. Their spread is method sensitivity, not a confidence interval. The complete Dirlik equations are in the white paper. Both assume stationary, Gaussian random response and linear, elastic dynamics.

5. Read the outputs

  • Damage ratio R = Iheritage/Irandom. Coverage ratio = R / requested damage factor. At least 1 means the modeled target is met at that oscillator.
  • Equivalent duration = total random duration × R. This is the random exposure giving the same modeled damage, with the same PSD shape and level. It is not remaining life.
  • Allowable amplitude scale = (R / target)1/b. The corresponding PSD scale is its square; exported per mode.
  • Peak ratio = maximum modeled heritage relative displacement / (k × random RMS relative displacement). This independently screens for peak severity. k is user-selected, not a stated probability or duration-based maximum.
  • Sensitivity matrix. The worst coverage ratio within the chosen scope is recalculated for each Q and b, using the same assumptions on both sides.

Peak-factor selection and response spectra

The random RMS spectrum is a relative-displacement VRS. The plotted random peak is the selected k times that RMS spectrum. A fixed k does not define an ERS with a stated event duration or non-exceedance probability. For an event maximum, use a justified extreme-value model and retain its duration, probability and bandwidth assumptions. A common leading narrowband estimate is √[2 ln(ν₀T)]; it is a typical extreme level, not a high-confidence bound. See Irvine, Peak Response to Random Vibration, Rev H for duration and probability distinctions.

Ground rules before a determination

  1. Verify the actual test article passed the heritage exposure; confirm records, axis, control points, dwell tracking, notches, tolerances and achieved levels.
  2. Establish configuration and mounting equivalence. Added mass, changed fasteners or boundary conditions can invalidate resonance and stress mapping.
  3. Use representative Q and fatigue slopes, then resolve sensitivity and modal-frequency uncertainty. Defaults are illustrative, not material allowables.
  4. Evaluate critical stresses or measured response when modal coupling, multiple hot spots, nonlinear joints, clearances, contact, fretting, solder fatigue, mean-stress shifts or multiaxial loading govern.
  5. Separate design qualification from the life of an already-tested article. Consumed life and future life add; qualification exposure is not a bank of remaining capacity.
  6. Document the peak check, numerical convergence, all coverage gaps, and the governing program's acceptance basis. This tool does not declare compliance with a standard.
